## Baseline comparison at approximately 90% target sparsity

All five seeds achieved 90.625% weight sparsity (6 active weights out of 64 prunable weights).

| Seed | Magnitude validation accuracy | Saliency validation accuracy | Saliency − magnitude |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 7 | 100.00% | 98.33% | −1.67 pp |
| 17 | 81.67% | 98.33% | +16.67 pp |
| 27 | 98.33% | 99.17% | +0.83 pp |
| 37 | 100.00% | 97.50% | −2.50 pp |
| 47 | 99.17% | 97.50% | −1.67 pp |
| **Mean** | **95.83%** | **98.17%** | **+2.33 pp** |

Across these five seeds, the population standard deviation of accuracy was 7.11 percentage points for magnitude pruning and 0.62 percentage points for saliency pruning. The standard deviation of the *paired per-seed differences* was 8.11 percentage points (sample SD). Saliency won on two seeds, lost on three, and one large magnitude-pruning failure drives much of the average difference.

**Interpretation:** these results are descriptive evidence on this small synthetic dataset, not proof that saliency is universally better or that the mean difference is statistically significant. Five seeds are a small sample; use more seeds and additional datasets before making a general claim.

## One falsifiable claim

> On the repository's current synthetic dataset and fixed five-seed protocol (7, 17, 27, 37, 47), at 90.625% achieved weight sparsity, mean held-out accuracy is 98.17% for Taylor-style saliency pruning and 95.83% for magnitude pruning, a paired mean difference of +2.33 percentage points.

This claim is falsified if rerunning the committed protocol with the same code, dataset, dependencies, and seeds does not reproduce those values within floating-point / deterministic-runtime tolerance. It does not claim a statistically significant or universal advantage.

## Real cost measurement and caveat

The committed `sparse_execution_benchmark.csv` records:

- Achieved sparsity: 90.625%.
- Weight-multiply-count proxy: 65,536 dense versus 6,144 active-weight multiplies for batch size 1,024 (90.625% fewer weight connections counted).
- Maximum absolute dense-versus-sparse-aware output error: 0.0 in the recorded run.
- Median latency: dense 0.1005 ms; sparse-aware 0.8458 ms.
- Dense/sparse-aware speed ratio: 0.119×. The sparse-aware implementation was approximately 8.42× slower in that environment.

The sparse-aware path actually skips exact-zero connections; it is not a dense matrix multiplication followed by masking. However, Python loops and indexing dominate this small model, so the lower multiply-count proxy did **not** translate into faster inference. Do not claim a latency speedup from these results. The multiply count is a structural proxy, not a hardware FLOP profiler, and excludes bias/activation work. Timings are environment-specific.

## Division-by-zero edge case

Tensor division now raises `ZeroDivisionError` before constructing a computation-graph node if the denominator contains any exact zero. This avoids silently creating `inf`/`nan` values that can poison later gradients. Regression tests cover scalar zero, a zero inside a denominator tensor, reverse division, and gradients with a broadcast denominator.
